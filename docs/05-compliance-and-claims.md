# Compliance and claims

This is a practical summary, not legal or tax advice. Check gov.uk and the ICO for current rules.

## Claims and advertising
- Every claim on the site must be true and provable. No fake testimonials, ratings, review counts, logos or statistics. UK advertising rules (ASA and CAP Code) require truthful, substantiated claims.
- Concept work must be labelled as concept designs for fictional businesses. Sample prices and reviews in concepts are sample content.
- Do not guarantee rankings, AI Overview placement, ChatGPT recommendations or sales. Promise activity you control: answered calls, tested assistants, delivered pages, measured reports.
- Essence Hair Treatment was built for his aunt's business, unpaid. Describe it honestly.

## Marketing rules (PECR, ICO) for outreach
Source: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/
- Email and SMS to individuals, including sole traders and some partnerships, generally need prior consent. Emails to limited companies and LLPs ("corporate subscribers") can go without consent, but you must identify yourself, include an opt-out in every message and comply with UK GDPR. Many electricians and builders are sole traders, so cold email to them is restricted.
- Live marketing calls: screen numbers against both the TPS and CTPS registers and your own do-not-call list; say who is calling; show your number. The ICO has fined firms tens of thousands of pounds for calling registered numbers.
- Check the ICO guidance on whether direct messages on social media count as electronic marketing before mass-messaging. Safest routes: warm introductions, in-person visits, replies to your content, and opt-in audits.
- At trade fairs, collect contact details with a clear opt-in and keep a record.

## UK GDPR and the AI assistants
- As a builder of chatbots you will usually be a data processor for the client. Use a data processing agreement, keep a list of sub-processors (model, voice, hosting providers) and tell clients what data goes where.
- Collect as little personal data as needed. Tell people they are talking to an AI and whether calls are recorded.
- Health data (dental, physio) is special-category data. Keep assistants to admin tasks (bookings, reminders, enquiries). No clinical advice. Human handover always. Do a DPIA before launch. Check the model provider's usage policy for health-related use.
- Never paste client customer data into free consumer chat tools. Use business or API terms.
- Model and platform costs are billed to the client at cost.

## Website compliance
- Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA. The site itself uses no tracking cookies. If analytics or embeds are added later, add a privacy notice and consent where required.
- Privacy notice and terms pages before launch. A contact route for data requests.
- Payments only through hosted checkouts (Stripe, Square, SumUp). Never handle card details.

## Contracts and risk
- Contract clauses: fixed scope and price, 50% deposit, two revision rounds, content deadlines, code and content ownership transfers on final payment, the AI can make mistakes, the client approves anything customer-facing, no guaranteed outcomes.
- Professional indemnity and public liability insurance. Tell the insurer about AI work.
- Revisit a limited company (with an accountant) before the first dental or physio client or once income and health-data risk grow.

## Parked: sole trader and HMRC (do not nag; raise before the first paid work)
- Register as a sole trader by registering for Self Assessment: https://www.gov.uk/become-sole-trader/register-sole-trader
- Must register if gross income (before expenses) from self-employment goes over £1,000 in a tax year (6 April to 5 April), by 5 October after that tax year ends. Registering early is free and lets him make voluntary Class 2 National Insurance payments. HMRC posts a UTR and activation code, usually within about 10 working days.
- Essence was unpaid (aunt's business), so nothing counts towards the £1,000 yet. Any thank-you payment might count, so check.
- Also: separate business bank account, records of every payment from day one, put aside a share of each payment for tax, ICO data protection fee self-assessment, insurance, contract template.
- HMRC Self Assessment helpline: 0300 200 3310.
