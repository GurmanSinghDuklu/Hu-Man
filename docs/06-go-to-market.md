# Go-to-market

## Principle
Show, then offer. Own the local search results for your own name, publish before/after work, and give every prospect something free that proves value: the presence audit.

## The free presence audit (lead magnet)
A 5-minute screen-recorded video plus a written list of fixes, covering: the site on a phone, page speed, the Google Business Profile, reviews and replies, and a check of the owner's main search in Google and ChatGPT (does the business appear, and who does?). Show a competitor appearing when they do not. Deliver in person, or to people who opted in, or to limited companies with an opt-out (see PECR rules in `05-compliance-and-claims.md`).

## Channels, in order
1. Warm network and discovery interviews (15 to 20 conversations across sectors).
2. Public before/after concept posts (labelled honestly) on LinkedIn, Instagram and Facebook groups; short transformation videos.
3. Own local search: Google Business Profile, the site, reviews from first clients.
4. Partners who see owners first: accountants and bookkeepers, photographers, sign makers and printers, bank business managers, commercial letting agents, trade wholesalers, practice-manager groups. Offer a referral fee or reciprocal arrangement.
5. Local networking: Bradford Chamber of Commerce, BNI, local business Facebook groups. Try to speak at an event.
6. Trade fairs. Attend as a visitor now and write down owners' exact complaints about their online presence (that becomes messaging). Exhibit later. Bring a tablet with a live before/after, a QR code to book an audit, and an opt-in form. Follow up within 48 hours. Consider a free "website and AI visibility clinic" through the Chamber before paying for a stand.
7. Paid ads only after a proven close rate and two or three sales.
8. Marketplaces (Fiverr, PeoplePerHour) only for early reviews.

## Outreach volume (targets, not guarantees)
About 100 targeted contacts a week across warm introductions, walk-ins, screened calls and consented messages. Expect roughly 8 to 10 conversations, three demos and one pilot. Cap new onboardings at about three a month at first.

## Discovery interview questions
1. Walk me through last week: what felt like a waste of your or your staff's time?
2. How many calls or enquiries do you miss in a normal week, and what happens to them?
3. How long from an enquiry to a quote or a booked appointment?
4. What do you chase people for?
5. If you got five hours back a week, what would you do with them?
6. What have you tried already, and what would you pay to fix this?

## Sample opening message (adapt; check PECR before sending cold)
"Hi [Name], I'm a Bradford web designer. I noticed [Business] doesn't have a booking option on your website. I put together a quick concept of how it could look (link). No obligation. Would a 10-minute chat this week suit?"

## Pilot offer
A 30-day paid pilot with one clear metric (for example "no call goes unanswered" or "every enquiry gets a reply within five minutes"). Promise activity, not sales.
